Key findings

Israel heads into October 27 elections amid four intertwined crises

  • Middle East Council on Global Affairs (Oct 7, 2026): Israel enters the final run-up to the October 27 parliamentary elections embroiled in four intertwined crises: (1) fragmentation in the ruling right-wing camp despite efforts to settle matters before the electoral list registration deadline; (2) an escalating rift between Israel and the Board of Peace overseeing the Trump plan for Gaza — a rift that has "practically frozen the plan to disarm Hamas until after the vote"; (3) accelerating settlement activity and an escalating security situation in the West Bank, which even the Israeli military establishment is warning could explode, already creating a diplomatic crisis with Western allies; and (4) a Lebanese front combining an American negotiating track based on "pilot zones" with continuing military escalation in southern Lebanon that is consolidating an approach of imposing facts on the ground prior to any settlement. Israel announced in early September it had achieved "full operational control" over the Ali al-Tahir heights in southern Lebanon, though the extent of this control remains disputed. Defense Minister Israel Katz has linked any withdrawal from the security zone to the complete disarmament of Hezbollah. (Source)

Netanyahu's pre-election security briefing

  • Times of Israel (Oct 19, 2026): Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu convened Israeli reporters at his Jerusalem office for his first in-person media briefing on security matters since the October 7, 2023 Hamas-led massacre. With Israelis heading to the polls on October 27, Netanyahu vowed to bring down the Iranian regime, downplayed the level of West Bank settler violence despite mounting international backlash, and warned that the election could determine whether Israel completes its military objectives or returns to what he characterized as failed security policies of the past. The central tenet of Netanyahu's updated national security doctrine: prioritizing preemptive offensive action over containment of threats, a shift he said was made necessary by October 7. The Iranian regime's nuclear ambitions remain chief among outstanding threats — "toppling the regime continues to be the key strategic objective, even after Israel's campaigns against Tehran in 2025 and 2026 failed to produce that outcome." On Gaza, Netanyahu reiterated that Israel would not permit reconstruction before Hamas is disarmed and the territory demilitarized, despite the Trump administration pushing to advance its 20-point plan for the Strip. Israel has agreed to a limited "Emirati bubble" in part of its security zone. On Lebanon, Netanyahu said Israel had allowed the Lebanese army to demonstrate its ability to clear Hezbollah infrastructure in two pilot areas but it had so far "not demonstrated sufficient capabilities." He suggested dismantling Hezbollah would open the door to a peace agreement with Beirut. Netanyahu warned of a limited window to advance objectives under mostly warm ties with Trump: "In two years, a president — male or female — could come to power in Washington who is very hostile to Israel." (Source)

Netanyahu vs. Eisenkot: election matchup

  • Jerusalem Post (Oct 2026): There are few major differences between Netanyahu and opposition leader Eisenkot on the Iranian threat — both will oppose a "bad deal" if the Trump administration opts for one, and both would be ready to attack Iran if the nuclear or ballistic missile threat became too close. Neither Netanyahu nor Eisenkot has a real plan for Gaza; both have promised to eliminate Hamas's control of the Strip but neither has a clear path. The IDF took control of 60%-75% of Gaza's territory in 2025-2026, and airstrikes several times a week continue to keep Hamas on the run, but the last two years have seen very little change in how close Hamas has come to losing control. On Lebanon, both would maintain a security zone and both are open to partial withdrawals in exchange for the Lebanese army partially clearing Hezbollah — neither trusts the Lebanese army to do its job sufficiently. Eisenkot has said he would draw a line at eight kilometers to prevent Hezbollah from firing anti-tank missiles into Israel. Stunningly, Netanyahu invoked a scenario in which Israel loses the US as an ally entirely — losing access to US-built aircraft including the F-35, various weapons, global diplomatic support, and potentially a major source of economic stability. This was interpreted as signaling that, given polling a week before the election, Trump would rather continue working with Netanyahu than get to know Eisenkot. (Source)
  • DW: Trump endorsed Netanyahu ahead of the elections. (Source)

Gaza: continued strikes despite ceasefire

  • Al Jazeera: Israeli air strikes continue to pound Gaza regularly despite a United States-brokered "ceasefire" in the besieged Strip. The UN and its partners are stepping up preparations for the approaching cold and rainy season even as attacks affecting civilians continue. Over the past two days, partners recorded multiple airstrikes, shelling, demolition blasts, naval fire, and shooting across all of Gaza's governorates, particularly west of the so-called "yellow line" established by Israeli authorities. Humanitarian operations are being squeezed by severe funding shortfalls — the 2026 Flash Appeal seeking more than $4 billion to support nearly 3 million people across Gaza and the West Bank is only 12% funded, with just $490 million received. UN Secretary-General António Guterres condemned the deadly shooting at the Islamic Center of San Diego. (Source)
  • ABC News (Oct 20): United Nations cuts Gaza food aid due to funding shortages; funding cuts are forcing the World Food Program to reduce lifesaving aid in Gaza. (Source)

Lebanon: military escalation and American negotiating track

  • MEC Council: The Lebanese front combines an American negotiating track based on "pilot zones" with continuing military escalation in southern Lebanon consolidating an approach of imposing facts on the ground prior to any settlement. Israel's announcement of "full operational control" over the Ali al-Tahir heights in southern Lebanon — though the extent of this control remains disputed — offers another example of how Israel leverages actions on the ground to strengthen its negotiating position. Defense Minister Israel Katz has linked any withdrawal from the security zone to the complete disarmament of Hezbollah. The question of Lebanon is likely to be a key priority for the next Israeli government, particularly given the current administration's insistence on linking any withdrawal to the complete disarmament of Hezbollah. (Source)
  • DW: Civilians in Lebanon hit hardest as Israel targets Hezbollah; as Israel expands operations and Hezbollah digs in, hopes for talks between Israel and Lebanon in Washington are fading. (Source)

West Bank settlement activity and Israeli isolation

  • MEC Council: Accelerating settlement activity and an escalating security situation in the West Bank, which even the Israeli military establishment is warning could explode, is already creating a diplomatic crisis with Israel's Western allies. Netanyahu downplayed the level of West Bank settler violence despite a mounting international backlash in his October 19 briefing. (Source)
  • Times of Israel: Netanyahu's October 19 briefing addressed Israel's growing isolation on the world stage. Opposition leader Lapid pledged to end Netanyahu's "policy of containment" by excluding Turkey and Qatar from Gaza's postwar arrangements, halting Qatari funds, eliminating Hamas's military capabilities and Hezbollah's remaining presence in southern Lebanon, and intensifying operations against Iran including strikes on its oil industry. (Source)
