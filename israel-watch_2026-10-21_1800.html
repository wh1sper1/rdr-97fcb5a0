Saved 2026-10-21 18:00 · Israel Watch · permanent link
Dossiers: Iran Middle East Israel Gaza Hamas West Bank Lebanon Hezbollah

Israel's war machine in 2026 is defined by the marriage of mass surveillance, artificial intelligence, and kinetic force at a scale that has no precedent in modern warfare. The systems built to surveil Palestinians — Unit 8200's harvesting of phone call metadata, the "Lavender" AI system that listed as many as 37,000 Palestinian men as linked to Hamas or Palestinian Islamic Jihad, the "Gospel" platform that churned out targets at unprecedented speed — have been deployed not only in Gaza but in the escalated 2024-2026 Iran-Israel conflict. When the U.S. and Israel conducted coordinated strikes against Iran beginning February 28, 2026, targeting senior political and military leadership, air defenses, and nuclear enrichment facilities, they relied on AI-assisted surveillance and targeting systems to intensify the attack — striking Iran roughly once every minute and a half at the height of the war.

The October 2026 revelation of "NAZA," a documentary by Oscar-winning Israeli filmmakers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, brought the inner workings of this system into stark public view. The film features testimony from 24 Israeli military intelligence officers and soldiers who spoke on condition of anonymity about their role in the surveillance and remote killing of Palestinian civilians in Gaza. The whistleblowers described a system in which hacked civilian phones were monitored so precisely that everyday phrases like "daddy's home" were used as triggers to launch airstrikes. The film received a 25-minute standing ovation at the Venice Film Festival. Israel's government condemned it as treason.

The cyber dimension of the conflict has intensified dramatically. Israel's National Cyber Directorate reported that the number of Iranian cyberattacks against Israel jumped from roughly 1,600 in June 2025 to some 4,800 in June 2026 — a threefold increase since the launch of the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran. Attacks were directed against systems used by Israel's critical infrastructure, central organizations, small- and medium-sized companies, and the public. Meanwhile, Israel-aligned hacking group Predatory Sparrow carried out cyberattacks on Iran's largest crypto exchange, Nobitex, destroying $90 million in cryptocurrency, and on Bank Sepah, one of the IRGC's principal banks, in June 2025.

The Lebanon front remains active. Hezbollah has deployed fiber-optic-guided first-person view drones that account for one-third of Israeli fatalities in southern Lebanon since March 2026. The Lebanese President said Israel has failed to comply with a U.S.-mediated framework agreement signed in June and continues daily violations in southern Lebanon. Israeli strikes on Hezbollah command centers in Nabatieh and Mayfadoun continue. The 2024 pager explosions — in which thousands of pagers and walkie-talkies used by Hezbollah members exploded simultaneously across Lebanon — were the product of a year-long operation in which Unit 8200 experts tested and refined methods for discreetly inserting explosive material during the manufacturing process.

Key findings

NAZA documentary: Israeli intelligence hacked phones and monitored Gaza families before strikes

A new documentary, "NAZA," directed by Oscar-winning filmmakers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, alleges that Israeli intelligence hacked phones and monitored Gaza families before military strikes, raising fresh questions about surveillance, targeting, and civilian safety. The film features testimony from 24 Israeli military intelligence officers and soldiers who spoke on condition of anonymity about their role in the surveillance or remote killing of Palestinian civilians in Gaza. Whistleblowers described how hacked civilian phones were monitored so precisely that everyday phrases like "daddy's home" were used as literal triggers to launch devastating airstrikes. The film received a 25-minute standing ovation at the Venice Film Festival. Israel's government called the accusations "completely false" and the filmmakers' claims unverifiable. (Source)

AI target systems: Lavender listed 37,000 Palestinian men as Hamas-linked

In April 2024, an investigation revealed "Lavender," an AI-based programme that, according to six intelligence officers, played a central role in the bombing of Gaza. Four officers said that at one stage early in the war it listed as many as 37,000 Palestinian men as linked to Hamas or Palestinian Islamic Jihad. The "Gospel" system similarly automated target identification using SIGINT, imagery, and historical data. In May 2021, Colonel Yoav's unit used AI to generate two hundred targets — a process that in the past would have taken almost a year. The Jerusalem Post dubbed Israel's May 2021 assault on Gaza "the world's first AI war." These tools — Lavender, Gospel, Depth of Wisdom, Alchemist, and Hunter — remained in use through 2026, leading Israeli soldiers to targets and guiding bombs at unprecedented rates. (Source)

Unit 8200: The cyber intelligence backbone of Israel's wars

Unit 8200, the IDF's elite signals intelligence and cyber warfare unit, has been central to Israel's long-running shadow war with Iran. In the escalated 2024-2026 phase of the Iran-Israel conflict, Unit 8200 integrated cyber and kinetic operations, supplying real-time SIGINT for Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear and missile facilities, disabling air defenses and command networks ahead of attacks. During Iran's April 2024 missile barrage and subsequent exchanges, Unit 8200 reportedly disrupted Iranian launch coordination, intercepted communications, and implanted persistent access tools in military networks. Cyber ops targeted ports, energy infrastructure, and telecoms, while AI-enhanced analysis tracked IRGC commanders. The unit has been linked to the Stuxnet, Flame, and Duqu cyber-attacks, and to the 2007 strike on the Syrian reactor at al-Kibar. (Source)

Iranian cyberattacks against Israel tripled since US-Israeli war began

Yossi Karadi, director general of Israel's National Cyber Directorate, told German newspaper Die Welt that in June 2025, during the first round of war between Israel and Iran, Israel's authorities registered around 1,600 hostile cyber incidents. During the same month in 2026, the number jumped to some 4,800 incidents — a threefold increase. The attacks were directed against systems used by Israel's critical infrastructure, central organizations, small- to medium-sized companies, and the public, including law practices and accounting firms. (Source)

Predatory Sparrow: Israel-aligned hackers destroy $90M in Iranian crypto

In June 2025, Israel-aligned hacking group Predatory Sparrow carried out cyberattacks on Iran's largest crypto exchange, Nobitex, destroying $90 million in cryptocurrency, and on Bank Sepah, one of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' principal banks. The attacks are part of a broader pattern of Israeli cyber operations targeting Iranian financial infrastructure, port systems, energy facilities, and telecommunications networks. (Source)

Pager attacks: Unit 8200's technical role in Operation Grim Beeper

In September 2024, thousands of pagers and walkie-talkies used by Hezbollah members exploded simultaneously across Lebanon and parts of Syria in a sophisticated Israeli intelligence operation. Unit 8200 played a critical technical role: according to Western security sources, the elite IDF signals intelligence and cyber warfare unit was involved in the development stage of the year-long operation. Unit 8200 experts tested and refined methods for discreetly inserting explosive material during the manufacturing process, ensuring the devices passed Hezbollah's inspections, including possible X-ray scans. The Mossad is credited with the overall planning and supply chain deception. (Source)

Iran claims no talks with US; Hezbollah deploys FPV drones in southern Lebanon

Lebanese President Joseph Aoun said Israel has failed to comply with a U.S.-mediated framework agreement signed in June and continues daily violations in southern Lebanon. Since March 2026, Hezbollah has deployed fiber-optic-guided first-person view drones that account for one-third of Israeli fatalities in southern Lebanon. The Israeli Air Force struck three Hezbollah command centers in the Nabatieh and Mayfadoun areas in southern Lebanon. Hezbollah continues to fire rockets and drones into Israel from Lebanon. (Source)

Handala hacktivists breach Israeli military media intelligence unit

Handala, a hacktivist group, broke into the Israeli military's Media Intelligence Monitoring and Analysis Unit and put more than 500 classified documents and tens of thousands of internal messages online. The breach represents one of the more significant hacktivist operations against Israeli military infrastructure in 2026. (Source)

West Bank: Israeli settlers attack Washington Post journalist in military-backed raid

Israeli settlers beat a Washington Post journalist in a military-backed West Bank raid. The men came down from a hilltop settlement shortly after 8 a.m. on a Friday, dressed in black, carrying slingshots and batons. At least one had a gun. By the time they finished, olive groves around Abu Falah, northeast of Ramallah, were burning, villagers were bleeding, and an American photographer for The Washington Post lay on the ground being kicked. The incident highlights the ongoing violence by settler groups in the West Bank, often with impunity. (Source)

UN: 340+ children killed in Gaza more than one year after ceasefire

A little over one year since a ceasefire agreement was announced between Hamas fighters and Israel, more than 340 children have been reported killed and hundreds more injured in Gaza, the UN Children's Fund (UNICEF) said on October 16, 2026. The figures underscore the ongoing human toll of the conflict even after the formal cessation of hostilities. (Source)

2026 Lebanon war: Israeli economic targeting of Hezbollah

Some of the earliest Israeli military efforts in March 2026 targeted Hezbollah's supply of gold and foreign currency, as well as branches of the Al-Qard Al-Hasan Association, a non-profit financial institution described as "Hezbollah's bank in Lebanon." The economic targeting was part of a broader strategy to degrade Hezbollah's financial infrastructure alongside kinetic operations in southern Lebanon. (Source)
