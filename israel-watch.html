October 2026 is the month the Israel wars entered a new documentation phase. The NAZA documentary — directed by Yuval Abraham and Rachel Shor, produced by The Guardian, and winner of the special jury prize at the Venice International Film Festival — presents testimony from 24 anonymous Israeli military and intelligence personnel alleging that AI-assisted targeting systems played a central role in the bombing of Gaza and that the military knowingly accepted large numbers of civilian casualties. One of the film's most striking reported allegations is that an IDF attack was approved in which as many as 500 civilians could be killed to eliminate a single Hamas target. The IDF has rejected the portrayal, calling that specific claim "completely false," and Netanyahu has called for an investigation into the soldiers and officers who spoke anonymously, urging the attorney general, military chief of staff, and head of Shin Bet to launch a comprehensive investigation into those involved for alleged security and defamation offences.

The throughline is the collision between Israel's military self-conception and the documentation emerging from within its own ranks. Unit 8200 — the IDF's signal intelligence arm, comparable in function to the NSA — has been linked to the Stuxnet, Flame, and Duqu cyber-attacks, the 2007 strike on the Syrian reactor at al-Kibar, and the 2024 pager attacks in Lebanon. Since 2014 it has faced growing criticism over mass surveillance of Palestinians and its role in AI-assisted targeting in Gaza. A joint investigation by The Guardian, +972 Magazine, and Local Call reported in August 2025 that Unit 8200 had used a customized area of Microsoft's Azure cloud platform to store audio files of millions of phone calls made by Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank — a trove used to plan airstrikes and as a basis for arrests in the West Bank. Microsoft said it was unaware its products had been used for surveillance of civilians, opened a review, and in September 2025 announced it had found evidence supporting elements of the reporting and had "ceased and disabled" specified subscriptions and services of a unit within the Israel Ministry of Defense.

What to watch next: the October 27 Israeli election — Netanyahu's fate and the direction of the next government will shape the trajectory of operations in Gaza, Lebanon, and the West Bank. The BBC Verify investigation identifying 254 incidents in which the IDF has been accused of killing or injuring people that it said it was investigating but have had no public outcome is a live accountability story. The ongoing Israeli airstrikes in southern Lebanon and the southern suburb of Beirut, combined with the settler violence against Palestinian olive harvesters in the West Bank (five killed and more than 100 injured in one week), make the ground situation across all three fronts a daily story.

The Israel story in October 2026 has three layers that are hard to separate but essential to track: the continuing violence in Gaza and the West Bank a full year after the October 2025 ceasefire (more than 1,400 Palestinians killed in Gaza since the truce, 1,135 verified by the UN Human Rights Office — 854 in reported Israeli jet and drone strikes alone); the explosive documentary NAZA, which premiered at Venice and won an Oscar, featuring anonymous testimony from 24 Israeli military intelligence insiders describing how the IDF calculated collateral damage in Gaza strikes; and the October 27 Israeli election, with Trump throwing his "Complete and Total Endorsement" behind Netanyahu.

The NAZA documentary is the most consequential cultural-political event in this cycle. Directed by Israeli filmmakers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor (the team behind the Oscar-winning No Other Land), the film features IDF soldiers and intelligence officers describing targeting practices that the filmmakers characterize as calculated acceptance of civilian deaths. Netanyahu has ordered the attorney general and Shin Bet head to reveal the identities of the soldiers interviewed and prosecute them for "harm to Israel's diplomatic and security interests." The film is in theaters in the UK and US and will be shown in more than 50 countries. That an Israeli government is trying to criminally expose the identities of its own soldiers who spoke to an Israeli filmmaker about their own military's conduct is a measure of how high the stakes have become.

What to watch next: the October 27 election result and whether Netanyahu's endorsement from Trump translates into votes; the UN Security Council's follow-up to its October 15 open debate on the Occupied Palestinian Territory; and whether the E1 settlement plan — which the UN Secretary-General has warned would deal a "devastating blow" to a viable contiguous Palestinian state — moves forward.

Key findings

More than 1,400 Palestinians killed in Gaza since ceasefire

UN News reported that more than 1,400 Palestinians have been killed in Gaza since the October 2025 Hamas-Israel ceasefire agreement that ended two years of war. The UN Human Rights Office verified 1,135 killings: 854 in reported Israeli jet and drone strikes, 163 by reported Israeli gunfire, and 118 in other circumstances. Ajith Sunghay, who heads the UN office, said, "Violence of this magnitude is outrageous at any time. To record it during a supposed ceasefire is simply unfathomable." Nearly all of Gaza's 2.1 million people still need shelter assistance, with IOM warning that current supplies meet only about 17% of needs. (UN News)

NAZA documentary: IDF soldiers describe calculated civilian deaths

MS NOW reported that NAZA, the new documentary by Oscar-winning director Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor, provides a searing look into the inner workings of the Israeli military's operation in Gaza. Its namesake is a Hebrew military term for "collateral damage." The film features testimony from 24 Israeli intelligence personnel and soldiers who described the orders they received and the strikes they carried out. Abraham said the film is based on investigative work done over three years with colleagues at The Guardian and Plus 972 magazine. The Guardian will make the film available for free on YouTube in early November. (MS NOW)

Netanyahu demands soldiers' identities be exposed

The Young Turks reported that Netanyahu has ordered Israel's attorney general and the head of the Shin Bet security service to reveal the identities of soldiers and officers interviewed in NAZA and prosecute them for causing "harm to Israel's diplomatic and security interests." The film, produced by The Guardian, details Israel's mass killing of civilians in Gaza and is in cinemas in the UK and US, with distribution in more than 50 countries. The Israeli military has denied everything the film says. (The Young Turks)

Israeli strikes across Gaza kill 4 on October 17

AP News reported that Israeli strikes killed four people across Gaza on October 17: two people and several others wounded east of Gaza City (Shifa hospital), a 14-year-old boy in Deir al-Balah (al-Aqsa hospital), and one person in Rafah (Red Cross field hospital). Israel's army said it struck Hamas and Islamic Jihad military operatives. The attacks came more than a year after the ceasefire deal, during which Israel continued to launch deadly strikes on the enclave. Since the ceasefire was announced in October 2025, Israeli strikes have killed more than 1,460 Palestinians in Gaza, according to Gaza's health ministry. (AP News)

West Bank settler violence surges during olive harvest

AP News reported that in Abu Falah on October 17, settlers attacked the village while Israeli military was seen raiding and throwing tear gas. Local officials and the Palestine Red Crescent Society reported attacks in at least eight villages in the northern West Bank. Israeli settlers attacked Palestinian farmers harvesting olives in Turmus Ayla, setting fire to cars and damaging property. Israel has imposed new rules prohibiting Palestinians from working in Area C, which encompasses the majority of the territory's agricultural land. At least four Palestinians have been killed and more than 100 injured since the harvest season began. The Palestinian Wall and Settlement Resistance Commission said settlers carried out 14,742 attacks in the West Bank since October 2023, killing 66 Palestinians. (AP News)

UN News reported that the Security Council held an open debate on October 15 on the current situation in the Occupied Palestinian Territory. Benjamin Netanyahu walked to the UN General Assembly podium in September and held up a pager. It was a reference to the September 17, 2024 operation in which thousands of pagers distributed to Hezbollah were detonated remotely — wounding more than 3,000 people and killing 12, including two children (PBS NewsHour). A head of government, on the world's stage, brandishing a weapon that maimed thousands of people in pockets, shops and homes as evidence of his competence. There has been no international investigation, no accounting for the children killed, and no mechanism by which any of it could be scrutinised — because the operation was covert, and covert operations are not auditable by design.

In the same speech he called allegations of genocide in Gaza "the biggest lie of the century," described West Bank settler violence as "a handful of young juvenile delinquents," and said no Palestinian state will be established while he leads Israel.

The aid pipeline for three million people is 12% funded

Whatever is decided at the ballot box on October 27, one number frames the week. The 2026 UN Flash Appeal seeking more than $4 billion to support nearly 3 million people across Gaza and the West Bank is 12% funded — roughly $490 million received (UN News, citing OCHA). This is not a funding shortfall in the abstract. It is governments choosing, year after year, to let a population survive on a fraction of what their own relief agencies say is needed, while the operations that created the displacement continue without condition.

The candidate asking for seven more years

Times of Israel's pre-election briefing records what Netanyahu is offering: a vow to bring down the Iranian government, a national-security doctrine that prioritises preemptive offensive action over containment — "We changed the Middle East because we changed ourselves" — a refusal to permit reconstruction in Gaza before Hamas is disarmed and the territory demilitarised, even as the Trump administration pushes a 20-point plan, and a dismissal of settler violence despite mounting international backlash. On Lebanon, he said the Lebanese army had shown it can clear Hezbollah infrastructure in two pilot areas but had "not demonstrated sufficient capabilities" — an answer that keeps the war open.

Middle East Eye's election explainer places the vote where the coverage of it should: the first election since 2022 and the attacks of 2023, the genocide case in Gaza and an ICC arrest warrant outstanding against the prime minister, the most right-wing government in the country's history, tens of thousands of Israelis displaced by Hezbollah fire, and an E1 settlement project east of Jerusalem that will cut the West Bank in two. Netanyahu is seeking a seventh term.

The polling has moved against him and stopped short of resolving anything. A Times of Israel / Zman Yisrael survey conducted October 14–15 (500 respondents, ±4.4%) puts opposition frontrunner Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar at 26 seats to Likud's 20, with the Jewish-led opposition bloc at 54 against 49 — short of the 61 needed, and short without the Arab parties, which several allied parties refuse to cooperate with (Times of Israel). Read that carefully: the alternative to the man with the arrest warrant is a former IDF chief of staff. The choice on offer is between two military postures, and neither bloc's arithmetic includes the Palestinian citizens whose representatives are treated as untouchable.

The warnings came from inside the house, and were dismissed

Three years after October 7, Times of Israel's blog desk reports what the state has never explained: Israeli intelligence obtained Hamas's detailed Jericho Wall invasion plan in 2022. Through 2023, female surveillance soldiers repeatedly reported Hamas exercises and reconnaissance along the Gaza border — and a specialist Unit 8200 analyst warned the exercises matched the invasion plan almost exactly. Military Intelligence dismissed the warnings, despite first-hand documented reporting. Former senior security commanders now warn the government's policies are creating the conditions for another avoidable confrontation.

That is the thread running through all of it: a population attacked while its own analysts' warnings were filed away, a prime minister who faces an arrest warrant rather than an inquiry, a covert weapon deployed against a supply chain with no accounting for the civilians it killed, and an election in which the question of who knew what, and when, has still not been answered.
