Key findings

Police misuse of Flock ALPR systems

  • USA TODAY (2026-08-28): Analysis of 75 million Flock searches across 3.5 years and 6,300+ agencies found hundreds of cases of officers searching single plates hundreds/thousands of times with vague justifications ("SUS", "TRAFFIC", "TEST", single letters). At least 19 police/sheriff employees in five states arrested, fired, or placed under investigation over a two-month span.
  • CNN (2026-07-26): Milwaukee officer Josue Ayala secretly used Flock to locate his romantic partner 124 times and the partner's ex 55 times; the detective who investigated him (Tehrangi Chapman) was later arrested for misusing the system himself. Institute for Justice tallied 2+ dozen cases of officers resigning or arrested for allegedly abusing Flock to stalk romantic interests.
  • ABC News: A Kentucky officer arrested for allegedly tracking his ex-girlfriend 2,000+ times; six Savannah, GA police employees fired in August 2026; a North Carolina officer arrested for tracking a boyfriend's ex-wife.
  • AJC (2026-08): Flock's self-audit tool ("audit assistance tool," alerts on abnormal searches) has itself triggered internal investigations and criminal charges — e.g., former Polk County officer Tracey Royston (4 misdemeanor charges), DeKalb County suspended eight employees, a Conyers dispatch supervisor arrested for 30+ non-law-enforcement accesses.
  • The Conversation: Washington Post investigation identified 50+ officers accused of misusing ALPRs (26 to spy on current/former partners), later updated to 100+ employees charged/accused. Braselton Police Chief Michael Steffman allegedly searched an ex's plates ~600 times; arrested Nov 2025, found dead before trial.

Federal / immigration angle

  • 2025: U.S. Customs and Border Protection accessed 80,000+ cameras during an undisclosed nationwide vehicle-tracking pilot — including one police department's cameras without its knowledge.
  • Public records from Danville, Illinois showed 4,000+ searches by federal agencies with potential immigration-enforcement focus, though ICE had no Flock contract.
  • 2026 study found 11,935 immigration-related searches in partial records from eight college police departments.
  • May 2025: A Texas sheriff's office searched 83,000+ cameras for a woman who had self-managed an abortion (per The Conversation/NPR).

Data capability concerns

  • Wired / 404 Media (Sept 2026): Analyzed data hackers copied from one Flock camera — ~21 days of logs: 50,200 vehicles, 1.6 million images. Software detected people and bicycles, isolated an American flag patch on a saddlebag.
  • 404 Media: "FreeForm" capability lets users search records by clothing, tattoos, or race — contradicting Flock's claim it only logs vehicles and doesn't do facial recognition.

Backlash / vandalism / cancellations

  • NPR (2026-08-21): Flock cameras vandalized in at least 36 states (sawed down, shot at, paint, stickers, stolen, rammed). DeFlock counts 100+ cities deactivating or canceling contracts.
  • ABC News: 50+ cities/counties canceled or deactivated ALPR cameras in 2026 (Politico tally). Cancellations include Fort Collins CO, Eugene OR, Madison WI, Knoxville TN, Syracuse NY, Walla Walla WA.
  • Flock response: New safeguards announced — every search must be labeled with a criminal case number; automatic review of abnormal search activity with proactive lockouts.

Notable quotes / data points

  • Gallup: 89% of Americans think government corruption is widespread (context item, see Trump file).
  • Institute for Justice: 100+ incidents of ALPR abuse identified (stalking, wrongful stops, non-law-enforcement use).
