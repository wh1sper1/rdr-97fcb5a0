TRENDING FIRST: The M-VAVE FM-1 firmware explosion is the biggest story in the synth world right now

M-VAVE officially opens FM-1 firmware to developers — October 20, 2026 (TODAY). M-VAVE has released "FM1 Open," an official firmware and development project that adds an eight-track sequencer, multiple synthesis engines, sample playback, and tools for developers to extend the FM-1. This is a huge deal: the FM-1, a budget-friendly FM synthesizer that became a viral hit, now has an official open firmware ecosystem. An interesting ecosystem of open solutions is emerging around the FM-1 — where you will soon be able to preview synths online, download them to cheap hardware, or run them as plugins in your DAW. In short order, the community has already produced alternate firmware that turns the FM-1 into a pocket Omnichord, and the FM-1 firmware scene is building at serious speed. This is the kind of grassroots hardware firmware movement that makes waves across forums, subreddits, and the entire synth community. (Source)

Elektron responds to the explosion of unofficial firmware — October 17, 2026. In his latest video, Matthew Piecora (EZBOT) shares his takeaways from an in-depth conversation with Elektron about the recent explosion of alternative firmware for their devices — a direct response to the growing community firmware movement that's been transforming Elektron's locked-down hardware. (Source)

Alternate firmware for Arturia Microfreak and Superfreaker now available — October 13, 2026. The Superfreaker promises to add Drive, Lo-Fi, a tape echo, a separate Release, Legato Glide, a metronome, transpose, Shift latch, and more. (Source)

Free firmware mod adds 'incredible' features to Elektron Model:Cycles — October 12, 2026. Model-TG is a free, open-source firmware mod that adds a full Sampler machine and a variety of other features — it's been getting major community attention with 18 comments on Synthtopia. (Source)

Big new gear announcements

Twisted Electrons FLEXFM — October 19, 2026. Twisted Electrons' upcoming FLEXFM combines eight synthesis-and-sampling tracks, analog filters, generative sequencing, and a joystick-based morphing system in a standalone hardware groovebox. This is a significant new hardware release generating buzz across the synth community. (Source)

First LOVE FM Synthesizer — available to order now! October 19, 2026. Nearly two and a half years after it debuted at Superbooth 24, the First LOVE FM is ready to order in two delicious colours. (Source)

Shear Electronics The Relic — October 18, 2026. The Relic pairs eight independent discrete analog voice cores with four-part multitimbrality, extensive modulation, and a continuously variable filter architecture — a major new analog polysynth. (Source)

Oddment ODD-1 — October 18, 2026. Oddment's ODD-1 hardware instrument and ODD-1v plug-in combine granular synthesis, real-time audio processing, and synchronized looping around a shared microsonic engine. (Source)

ACME Synthworks XP60 — October 20, 2026. ACME Synthworks' XP60 brings the six-voice analog architecture and deep modulation system of the Oberheim Xpander to a compact desktop instrument, with updated controls and display options — a modern take on a classic analog design. (Source)

Future Retro 777 — first jam posted October 18, 2026. Synthesist Nathan King (zafrosoundz) shared a synth jam featuring the new Future Retro 777 — a reissue of the classic bassline synthesizer. (Source)

Stylophone Mini — turns the classic pocket synth into a keychain. The Stylophone Mini Original packs a playable metal keyboard, built-in speaker, and vibrato effect into a tiny instrument designed to travel on a keyring or bag. (Source)

Scene buzz

The M-VAVE FM-1 firmware scene is absolutely buzzing right now — the combination of an affordable, viral hardware synth getting official open firmware support, plus a rapidly growing ecosystem of community firmware mods (FM-1 as a pocket Omnichord, FM-1 alternate firmware previewable in a web browser), has the synth community genuinely excited. This is the kind of grassroots hardware movement that defines scene moments. Simultaneously, the Teenage Engineering founder's statement about quitting synthesizer production has the community reeling — the OP-1 is one of the most iconic and influential synths of the last decade, and the news has sparked intense discussion about the future of boutique hardware synthesis. The firmware modding movement is also gaining mainstream recognition, with Elektron officially responding to the explosion of unofficial firmware for their devices — a sign that the community's creativity is now impossible for manufacturers to ignore. (Source)
